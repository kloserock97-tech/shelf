A contents capsule for long pages

One floating pill shows where you are and how much is left. Tap it for the whole map, deep dives included.

01In brief

Long pages need a map, not three widgets. The capsule names the section you are reading, fills a ring as you go and opens the table of contents as a sheet.

02Context

Readers get lost in the middle.

A case study, a guide or a report runs for several screens. Halfway down the title is gone, the menu is gone, and so is any sense of how much is left.

The usual fix is a stack of helpers: a sticky sidebar, a progress bar and a back-to-top button. Each one is fine alone. Together they take a whole column of the page and give the eye three places to look.

03Research

People skim headings, then jump.

Almost nobody reads a long page top to bottom. People scroll fast, stop at a heading that sounds relevant, read a little and move on. Many scroll back up later to find the part they skipped.

  • Scroll fastThe first half of the page goes by in seconds, headings only.
  • Stop at headingsA clear heading is where reading actually starts.
  • Come backThe way back up is as important as the way down.

04Decisions

One control instead of three.

The capsule replaces the sidebar, the progress bar and the back-to-top button. It floats at the bottom centre, where a thumb reaches it and where it never covers the text column.

Deep dives

01Spy by positionWhy the current section is measured, not observed

The current section is the last heading that has risen above a line 150 px from the top. It is recalculated on scroll, once per frame.

An IntersectionObserver misses one case: after a jump from the contents the heading stops right under the top edge and never crosses the observer's band.

02Nested entriesDeep dives open in place

Long explanations live in collapsed blocks inside their section. In the contents they are nested under it: picking one opens the block and scrolls to it.

Opening a block by hand puts its id in the address, so a shared link lands on an open deep dive.

03One sheet, every widthA panel on desktop, a bottom sheet on phones

On a wide screen the contents rise as a panel above the capsule. Below 760 px the same list becomes a bottom sheet from edge to edge.

Escape, the close button or a tap outside close it, and focus goes back to the capsule.

05Outcome

Less chrome, the same map.

The text column got its full width back. The contents are one tap away on any screen, and the ring answers "how much is left" without a single number.

06Next steps

What could come next.

  • Remember the placeBring returning readers back to where they stopped.
  • Minutes leftOn very long pages, time can say more than a ring.
  • One keyOpen the contents from the keyboard without hunting for the pill.

07Takeaways

A long page needs one honest answer to "where am I", not three widgets that each answer part of it.
